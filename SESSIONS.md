# Paper sessions — sanitized summaries

What is published about each session: its UTC window, the preset and slow-brain frame, the slots planned and attempted, the number of switches, the result at the virtual capital against holding the starting position over the same window, and how the session ended. What is not published: the ledgers, logs and reports themselves, the call counts and costs of either model, their latencies, their answer distributions, hostnames and configuration hashes. Zero switches is a result of the voting rule, not a measurement of any model.

Two kinds of rows. **Sessions 1–8 are paper sessions on the live tape:** paper mode, the starting position in ETH, on-chain quotes from Uniswap v3 on Base, the Coinbase ETH-USD tape, and, unless the row says otherwise, a virtual capital of 1,000 USD and a 60-second tick (session 8 runs four sizes and one five-minute arm). **Sessions 9–14 are historical replays, a different kind of evidence:** the same engine and judge over Coinbase candles under a simulated clock, 10,000 USD, a five-minute tick, the judge alone without the slow brain, the date hidden from it, and scenario fills in place of on-chain quotes (README: Replay). Every replay window is a separate start in ETH, several of them overlap (the FTX month lies inside the seven months of 2022, February 2024 inside the rise), and their results do not add up to one portfolio. "Hold" is the value of keeping the starting position for the same window: a diagnostic benchmark, not a strategy. Results are mark-to-market at the end of the window after the paper execution costs (quote, slippage, estimated gas); the inference bills are booked separately and are small against the capital; a replay row is net of its judge calls as well. Sessions 1–3 and 6 ran on a Windows laptop, 4–5 and 7–14 on a macOS laptop.

Notes.

- Sessions 1–3 used the rule now called `forecast`: no judgment qualified as a vote, so there was nothing to switch. Session 2 stopped on the first judge reply whose bill could not be established, which is what the guard is for; session 3 ran with the paper-only continuation enabled and its three unresolved bills reserved.
- Sessions 4 and 5 ran several arms side by side over the same market hours. The named setting was the intended difference; separate feeds, quote requests and API timing gave the arms different inputs, so these are exploratory comparisons rather than controlled identical-input trials. Every arm made the same two switches: it sold ETH near a local low and bought it back near a local high. That is the whipsaw cost of a regime flip, and it is why every trend arm ended below "hold".
- Session 5 was launched three times. The first two launches (13:30 and 14:59 UTC) halted at the first switch, when the public RPC endpoint answered the quote with `missing revert data` while several arms switched at once; the retry and deadline logic was changed between launches, and the third launch ran its full 300 slots. The halted ledgers are kept privately as part of the record.
- In session 5 the slow brain in its forecast frame vetoed most buy-backs and only delayed the same switch; without it (5b) and in the regime frame (5c) the outcome was the same two switches. The default frame stays `forecast`; the lever that would change these results is not the slow brain but the judge's regime call flipping at the edges of a sideways corridor.
- Hold differs slightly between the arms of session 5 because each arm values its window from its own first and last tick.
- "Worst drop" is the maximum drawdown of the arm's wallet over the window against the maximum drawdown of holding ETH over the same window, computed from the ledger's price series; it is reported from session 7 on, after a reader pointed out that a hedge should be judged on the drop it sits out rather than on the return.
- Session 7 tested a reader's suggestion: agreeing votes a minute apart are one opinion repeated, so a switch should depend on new information. The night was a 1 %-wide corridor (ETH 2 657–2 684). The control sold at 2 665 and bought back at 2 684 near the top, the same whipsaw as in sessions 4 and 5, 0.8 points behind hold. The breakout arm vetoed 175 candidates because the price never moved 0.9 % beyond the 2-hour range, made no switch and ended exactly at hold. The persistence arm sold once and never saw 18 of 20 minutes agree on ETH again, so it sat out the dip with a smaller drawdown and missed the recovery. The bar was written down before the run: a flip has to be worth more than 0.9 %. Two slots per arm were missed during a two-minute network drop.
- Session 8 ran the breakout strategy at four virtual sizes and at a five-minute tick over the same ten hours, to see the pool's price impact in live fills. The day was flat (a 1.2 % band, 77–83 of 600 judgments "trend down", no run of five) and no arm switched, so no fill was observed; the size question stays answered by the quote probe of 2026-10-02 (a round trip costs −0.10 % at $1 000, −0.13 % at $10 000, −0.36 % at $100 000, −2.8 % at $1 000 000 on this pool). The judge's account ran out of credit from 14:04 to 15:03 UTC: 56–59 ticks per arm have a judge error of that kind, the engine treated each as a lost judgment with an unknown bill, and the sessions' net result is therefore reported as unresolved although nothing was served or charged in that hour; the gross result is the line above. The five-minute arm behaved like the others and is the tick the replays of session 9 use.
- Session 9 is a replay, not a live tape: the same engine, vote rule, filters, limits and judge over Coinbase candles under a simulated clock (README: Replay). Three windows chosen for what a hedge is for and what it costs: a top followed by a crash, a collapse, a rally. The fill is the open of the minute after the decision under the cost scenario measured on 2026-10-02 (the pool fee plus the price impact at $10 000: 1.3 basis points one way); the state's date line is hidden from the judge, its price level is not, so these are a screening with that caveat. No slow brain. A latched daily loss is lifted at the next simulated midnight, since no owner sits at a replay; the kill limit is not. Two things went wrong and were fixed before the rows above: at a five-minute tick on candle boundaries the breakout range held the candle the price was printed in and vetoed every candidate (fixed, every breakout arm rerun), and the judge's account ran out of credit for an hour (every affected arm rerun in full). What the rows show: the votes-only rule (9a, 9d, 9g) switches 60–95 times a month, caught both the rise and the crash of May 2021, fell with ETH in November 2022 and gave up more than half of the February 2024 rally; the breakout condition makes it quiet (no switch in the rally, so no cost there) but on its own does not save it, because the 3 % daily stop latches before the votes line up and then blocks the exit (9e); the same stop kept 9b from churning through the crash of 19.05 that 9c, with a 10 % stop, churned through to the kill limit. The lesson is not a wider stop: a loss latch should block entries into ETH, not exits into USDC (the `RISK_LATCH_BLOCKS_EXITS` knob, tested in session 10). Numbers are sampled properties of one run per arm with a stochastic judge; a second run would not reproduce them exactly.
- Session 10 is the long replay the hedge is judged by: seven months of 2022 with two collapses and the rallies between them, three arms, 61 632 five-minute slots each (about 24 800 seconds of wall time and $3.2 of judge calls per arm). Holding ETH lost 52.5 % over the window with a worst drop of 70 %. The votes-only rule (10a) ended three points ahead of holding after 130 switches, and only because the kill limit froze it in ETH from 10.06, so it rode the recovery; the breakout arm (10b) ended 42 points ahead of holding with six switches, and it too was frozen by the kill limit from 19.06 after an early re-entry; the arm allowed to exit under a latched loss (10c) was never halted, stood at −9.6 % on the day ETH bottomed where holding stood at −67 %, and then gave back 25 points of that lead in whipsaw through the summer rallies, ending 36 points ahead of holding. Two conclusions we hold to: the 3 % daily stop and the 30 % kill limit are the wrong shape for a hedge, since both block the exit the hedge exists for (and, by freezing an arm, can also flatter it by accident); and the breakout exits are late while the re-entries are early, which is where the next change belongs. The same window run once before, stopped on 19.07 by a slow ledger (fixed since: two indexes), agreed in direction with a different judge draw: −38 %, +9 % and +33 % against −43 % for holding at that date. Replay caveats as in session 9. Since agent 2.12.0 the settings of 10c — the 0.9 % breakout over two hours and a loss latch that blocks only entries into ETH — are the defaults; 10a is `BREAKOUT_MIN_PCT=0 RISK_LATCH_BLOCKS_EXITS=true`, 10b the breakout with the strict latch (`RISK_LATCH_BLOCKS_EXITS=true`).
- Session 11 replays the other half of what a hedge has to survive: six months of a rise, October 2023 to April 2024, with the arm that ended session 10 best on the day ETH bottomed. It sold once on the way up (14.11.2023, a 0.9 % break below the two-hour low at 1 953) and then stayed in USDC for three and a half months while ETH went from 2 000 to 3 200: the judge called the trend up thousands of times, but its way back needs the price 0.9 % above the high of the last two hours at the same moment five of six judgments agree, and a slow, steady rise rarely prints such a burst (10 075 candidate-ticks were vetoed by the breakout condition). It came back on 27.02 at 3 273 and sold again on 05.03 at 3 591. Result: +28 % against holding's +118 %, with a worst drop of 12 % against 25 %. The insurance is cheap in a crash and in a sharp rally (session 9, February 2024: no switch), and expensive in a slow rise. That is the case the re-entry rules (`REENTRY`, README: Strategies) are for. Replay caveats as in session 9.
- Session 12 is the rise of session 11 replayed with the other three strategies, so the four bots stand on one window. The judge's votes alone (12a) switched 610 times in six months and ended below the start while ETH more than doubled: every false flip paid, and the daily latch lifted eleven times. The breakout with the strict latch (12b) made three switches and +80 %, but its sale on 05.03 at 3 591 was never followed by a re-entry, so it ended the window in USDC. Bot 4 (12c), which comes back 0.9 % above its last sale without waiting for votes, made six round trips of a day each and ended at +104 % against +118 % for holding: the insurance in a rise cost 14 points instead of session 11's 90.
- Session 13 is bot 4 over the seven months of 2022: one sale on 05.05 at 2 753. Five minutes later the price was back above the re-entry level and the buy-back was blocked by the day's latched loss limit and the minimum hold; no later tick saw the level, so the bot sat in USDC through Luna, the June low and FTX and ended at +0.9 % against −52.5 % for holding, with a worst drop of 7 % against 70 %. It is the same exit as session 10's hedge arm (10c, −16.5 %) without that arm's 25 whipsaw switches through the summer rallies: the re-entry rule, not the exit, made the difference.
- Session 14 repeats the three single months of session 9 with bots 3 and 4. In the FTX month both sold at the same instant (08.11 04:15 at 1 468) and stayed out, bot 3 because its way back needs a breakout and bot 4 because the day's latched loss limit blocked its buy-back: −6.6 % against −17.7 %. In the February 2024 rally neither switched. May 2021 is the month bot 4 loses: a choppy top where every 0.9 % dip was a sale and every buy-back came 1.5–7 % above it, six round trips and −13.7 % against −2.5 % for holding, while bot 3, which needs a breakout to come back, made +14.9 %. Across the five windows bot 4 beat holding in three, matched it in one and lost in one; its worst window against holding is −14 points (the rise) where bot 3's is −90. That is why bot 4 is the default from 2.15.0 and bot 3 stays one line away (`STRATEGY=hedge`). The nine arms cost about $14 of judge calls. Replay caveats as in session 9; every number is one draw of a stochastic judge per arm.
- Reading bot 4's rows (12c, 13, 14b, 14d); added 2026-10-05, after the write-up. Three things the rows do not say on their own. First, the windows are separate starts, each with $10 000 in ETH, and their results do not add up: a bot 4 that ran without a stop would not have been in ETH at the start of the rise. Its way back needs only the price, so its path between a sale and that level follows from the candles. After the sale of 05.05.2022 (fill 2 753, level 2 778) the price touched the level for the last time half an hour later; to the end of that window it stayed under it, between the windows its highest was 2 143 (16.04.2023, Coinbase daily candles), and the first five-minute tick at or above the level is 14.02.2024 21:55, 650 days after the sale. Bought back there at 2 784 with 1.2 % less ETH than at the start, and with no sale in the replay of the rise over the same weeks, the bot ends 01.04.2024 at +32.0 % against +33.7 % for holding. On the way holding stood at −67 % (the June 2022 low) and the bot at +1 %; its worst drop was 7 % before the return and 25 % after it, holding's 70 %. Over the rise window alone that is +31 %, not the +104 % of 12c, which starts in ETH. This is computed from the rule and the two replays, not replayed as one run: the ten months between the windows have no judge record, and none is needed while the bot waits in USDC under its level. Bot 4 never buys back below its own sale: every completed round trip cost it ETH (1.0–1.5 % in the rise, 1.4–6.5 % in May 2021), and its lead over holding exists only while the price is under that sale. It is insurance against the fall, not a way to end with more than holding. Second, the daily loss limit is part of bot 4's result. A latched daily loss blocks buying back until it is lifted, and in both crash windows that is what kept the bot out, not the price: on 05.05.2022 the one buy-back candidate of the whole window came five minutes after the sale and was blocked; on 08.11.2022 the price stood above the level on 77 ticks between 04:30 and 17:50, every one blocked, before the collapse of that evening. Screened with the recorded answers (README: screening a rule change for free; not paid replays): with the daily limit at 10 % the FTX month ends at −20.6 %, below holding's −17.7 %, the rise at +96.7 % and May 2021 at −12.5 %; the 2022 window keeps its single sale while the 20-minute minimum hold stands (the blocked candidate was inside it) and ends at −30.8 % on the kill limit when the hold is cut to five minutes as well. The minimum hold alone, from 5 to 60 minutes, changes no window by more than half a point. The same latch is the cost of May 2021: five of the six buy-backs waited for midnight. Third, "no halt" in rows 10c–14 means no kill limit and no halt left at the end. The daily loss limit did latch, and the replay's midnight rule lifted it 44 times in 10c, 4 in 11, 11 / 18 / 32 in 12a–c, 2 in 13, 8 / 13 in 14a–b, 3 / 3 in 14c–d and once each in 14e–f. Live, each of those waits for the operator's `--reset-halt`.
- Session 15, the bar, written on 2026-10-05 before the run and before looking at the month's prices. A reader pointed out that bot 4 was picked after seeing the five windows above, so a month none of the four bots has run on is its first real test. The month is September 2026: the latest complete calendar month, chosen by that rule alone, and later than any model's training. All four bots, the settings of sessions 9–14. What would count: (1) bot 4's worst drop is not deeper than holding's by more than one point; (2) if the lowest price of the month is less than 15 % under its opening price, bot 4 ends no more than 5 points behind holding; if the fall is deeper, bot 4's worst drop is at most half of holding's; (3) against holding, bot 4 is not worse than bot 3 by more than 2 points and is not the worst of the four. All three: the choice of bot 4 held on this month. If none of bots 2–4 switches, the month says nothing either way. One month is one draw, and the rows are published whatever they show.
